01UF ICIC Lab · Undergraduate researcher

Dexterous exoskeleton + multimodal data

A modified 12-DOF hand exoskeleton integrating tactile sensing, camera observations, wrist pose and encoder feedback for multimodal dexterous-manipulation research.

Mechanical and sensor integration

Modified and integrated a 12-DOF hand exoskeleton with tactile sensors, cameras, encoders and embedded electronics. Recalibrated sensors, diagnosed and repaired electromechanical faults, and maintained the multimodal data-acquisition system during human-subject experiments.

Experimental scope20 participants · 4 tasks

Executed data collection and real-time system monitoring for a 20-participant study across four manipulation tasks. Synchronized and preprocessed tactile, visual, wrist-pose and encoder streams comprising 1,215 demonstrations and 204,586 aligned samples across 31 recording sessions.

Research analysis and communication

Generated publication figures from processed datasets, coauthored two IEEE ICRA 2027 submissions on adaptive exoskeleton design and cross-hand tactile supervision, and independently presented tactile-sensing and edge-AI research to the Office of Naval Research.

Manuscripts are submitted, not accepted publications. This is collaborative lab research. The exoskeleton work adapted the original DexUMI project; credit for the original framework and design belongs to its authors.

02UF Bio-Sensing, Automation & Intelligence Lab · Undergraduate researcher

Selective blueberry harvesting end effector

Developing a modular, arm-mounted end effector for selective harvesting inside dense blueberry canopies, with parametric mechanism design and physics-based evaluation before fabrication.

Initial prototype and drivetrain recovery

Restored an earlier DYNAMIXEL-driven gripper by redesigning its failed servo-to-gear interface. Implemented Python control of actuator travel and speed, establishing a functional mechanical baseline for the next end-effector iteration.

Parametric end-effector redesign

Developing parametric CAD for synchronized catch jaws, independently compliant spring-loaded teeth, interchangeable tooth-bank cartridges and integrated fruit-collection channels. Evaluating packaging, assembly clearances and retaining geometry to selectively detach ripe blueberries while limiting contact with unripe fruit and surrounding stems.

Python/MuJoCo validation

Developing a physics-based simulator of compliant stems, spring-loaded teeth, fruit contact and attachment release. Using force and motion diagnostics to refine retaining geometry, while extending the environment toward realistic blueberry bushes with mixed-ripeness clusters for testing selective detachment, collision and failure modes before physical prototyping.

Current statusModular end-effector CAD in progress.

These open and closed configurations document an in-progress mechanical design, not a fabricated or validated autonomous harvesting system.

CAD preview includes 341 modeled bodies; one tooth-bank layout remains an earlier proxy. Flexible hose, fitted actuator and robot arm are not modeled. The full-bush simulator, selective harvesting and field deployment remain future validation goals.

03Independent project · Design, electronics & testing

Wearable robotic third thumb

An iterative tendon-driven robotic augmentation project combining 3D-printed mechanism design, dual-servo ESP32 control, bearing-supported joints and spring-assisted return.

Hardware integration and baseline

Replicated and adapted the open-source Third Thumb Project, integrating a 3D-printed tendon-driven digit, dual servos, ESP32-based control, Bowden-cable transmission and soldered power circuitry. Bench-tested closure under load to establish baseline mechanical performance.

Failure analysis and mechanical redesign

Identified insufficient load-bearing rigidity and unreliable reopening after closure in the original rubber-band return architecture. Iterated printed joint housings, shaft supports, fastener interfaces and cable routing in parametric CAD, applying motion-clearance checks and print-layer inspection to improve fit, load transfer and manufacturability.

Second-generation actuation architecture

Prototyped a reinforced, bearing-supported linkage with offset joint geometry and spring-loaded return. Closing the joint increases spring compression; releasing tendon tension permits the restoring force to drive reopening. Independent yaw/curl motion and opposing yaw-tendon routing are under development. Initial tests demonstrated spring-assisted return, but reliable response across joint orientations remains unresolved.

Prototype 02 → Prototype 03Spring-assisted return demonstrated.
Wearable redesign in progress.

Prototype 02 demonstrates the spring-return concept, but return response varies with joint orientation and the present assembly is too bulky for hand mounting. Prototype 03 will focus on compact packaging, joint geometry, tendon routing and repeatable actuation while preserving improved structural rigidity.

This project is unfinished. The pictured bench prototype is not wearable yet; EMG control and tactile sensing are potential future extensions, not demonstrated functions. Original open-source design credited to laxmangordon.

From failure to learned motion

Reproducing a humanoid locomotion baseline and evaluating robotic manipulation—with reproducible experiments and explicit failure analysis.

Simulation and policy-training pipeline

Built a reproducible AWS EC2 workflow using MuJoCo Playground, JAX and Brax PPO for Unitree G1 locomotion and Franka Panda manipulation. Trained a G1 policy over 202M+ simulated transitions and evaluated 16 environment configurations spanning reward shaping, curricula, reset conditions and reference tracking.

Franka Panda · manipulation

Separating progress from task completion

Later v4 evaluation · 60 episodes / 3 seeds

Grasp and lift succeeded; final placement did not.

Across 60 randomized simulation episodes and three evaluation seeds, the later curriculum achieved 100% lift-and-hold performance but 0% verified stable placement. A separate 85% near-target release condition did not satisfy the final-state placement criterion because the target lacked physical support. Evaluation distinguished successful intermediate manipulation from completed task execution.

The video above shows v3; the reported 60-episode metrics describe the later v4 evaluation. All results are in simulation. This work uses existing RL algorithms rather than claiming a new algorithm.
