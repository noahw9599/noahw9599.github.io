02UF Bio-Sensing, Automation & Intelligence Lab

Selective blueberry harvesting grasper

A custom end effector with synchronized, compliant dual-bowl grippers, designed for working around delicate fruit and crowded branches.

My contribution

Designed and iterated the geared mechanism, integrated a ROBOTIS DYNAMIXEL servo and 12 V power, and wrote Python control scripts for travel limits, speed, torque, and recovery procedures.

The engineering problem

Binding and insufficient torque prevented consistent motion. I diagnosed the drivetrain, redesigned components, and FDM-printed critical parts in ASA to improve alignment and torque transfer.

Initial functional check10 consecutive cycles

Validated repeatable opening and closing across 20 commanded movements after mechanical iteration.

A bench-level functional result, not an endurance test or a demonstrated autonomous harvest. Perception and robot-arm integration remain broader project work.

Mechanism decisions and iteration +

Synchronized motion

A geared linkage couples the two sides of the grasper. The paired bowls provide a coordinated closing motion around the target.

Mechanical iteration

Friction, alignment, and load-bearing component stiffness affect the torque that reaches the contact surfaces. Debugging focused on the drivetrain, not just increasing the motor command.

03Independent project · Design, electronics & testing

Wearable robotic third thumb

An iterative robotic augmentation project: adapting an open-source thumb, diagnosing mechanical failures, and redesigning its return mechanism for greater strength and reliability.

Replication & adaptation

Replicated the open-source Third Thumb Project and modified the mechanism and electronics for my available hardware, integrating dual-servo, ESP32-based Bowden-cable actuation. The initial build provided a baseline for testing and redesign.

Failure diagnosis

Testing exposed two central weaknesses: the original construction was insufficiently robust for practical manipulation, and its rubber-band return could not reliably reopen the thumb after closure. Rather than increasing actuator power alone, I redesigned the mechanical system around load-bearing joints and a more dependable return mechanism.

Second prototype

Rebuilt the assembly with a stronger printed structure, bearings, and offset joint geometry that increases spring compression as the joint closes. When motor tension is released, the compressed spring provides a restoring force toward the resting position. This spring-assisted bearing mechanism works in initial proof-of-concept testing, but still requires tuning across joint orientations.

Prototype 02 → Prototype 03Spring-assisted return demonstrated.
Wearable redesign in progress.

The second prototype validates the return mechanism concept, but spring response varies with joint orientation and the assembly remains too large and bulky to fit on a hand. A third prototype will target compactness, joint tuning, and reliable motion while retaining the strength improvements.

This project is unfinished. The pictured bench prototype is not wearable yet; EMG control and tactile sensing are potential future extensions, not demonstrated functions. Original open-source design credited to laxmangordon.
